## 5. Daily life inside — mobility — **[VERIFIER: paper confirmed from full text; one quote re-attributed]**

## 6. Daily life inside — light, air, fire — **[VERIFIER: sunlight CORRECTED; evacuation unverifiable]**

- Canyon geometry: 200 m wide, 500 m walls → the sun must be **above 68.2°** *in the plane perpendicular to the canyon* to reach the far edge of the floor. At ~28.1° N noon sun is **38.5° (winter solstice), 61.9° (equinox), 85.3° (summer solstice)** **[COMPUTED ✓]**.
- **[VERIFIER: CORRECTED]** The previous statement "~3 h of direct sun/day in midsummer and none for roughly half the year" mixed two different quantities. Recomputed for an idealised straight, unobstructed canyon (minute-by-minute solar position, 28.1° N):
  - **Noon sun reaches the floor only from ~7 April to ~5 September (151 days); for ~214 days a year (early Sept → early April) the floor gets no midday sun.**
  - For a true **east–west** axis the floor receives **no direct sun at all on ~183 days a year (0 h at the equinoxes and in winter)**, but in midsummer low morning/evening sun shining *along* the canyon axis lights the floor for **~8–9 h/day** (the "3 h" figure counts only sun higher than 68.2° regardless of direction, which is the wrong criterion for a long canyon).
  - The real alignment is closer to **WSW–ENE (~20° off E–W)**; with that axis the floor gets some low-angle sun on ~280 days but **still none around the winter solstice**, and only ~3.5 h at the equinoxes.
  - Sky-bridges, interior floors and the "vertical layering" would shade the floor further, not less. Safe caption: **"겨울 반년, 바닥에는 정오 햇빛이 닿지 않는다"** rather than "하루 3시간".
- Critics: "No ray of light would ever reach the ground in the narrow canyon… massive energy consumption for artificial lighting, air conditioning" — [Lampoon Magazine, 2025-03-24](https://lampoonmagazine.com/article/2025/03/24/the-line-city-project-saudi-arabia-neom-sustainable-architecture-critics-dystopian-future/) **[SOURCED, unverifiable live; the geometry above shows this is an overstatement for summer]**; NEOM's counter: the 200 m width is "intentional… allowing natural light to penetrate from the sides" — [ArchDaily, The Line at a Crossroads](https://www.archdaily.com/1039911/the-line-at-a-crossroads-revisiting-neoms-vision-for-a-utopian-city) **[SOURCED, unverifiable live]**
- Evacuation: descending 160 flights takes a healthy adult "well over an hour"; stair descent is >70 % of total evacuation time in super-high-rises; Burj Khalifa and Lotte World Tower rely on evacuation elevators and "achieving the one-hour target remains a challenge in complex, high-occupancy environments" — [MDPI Buildings 14(10):3164, 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/14/10/3164); [SkySaver Burj Khalifa evacuation](https://skysaver.com/blog/burj-khalifa-evacuation-plan-how-it-works/) **[SOURCED] [VERIFIER: unverifiable — neither page openable; the paper's existence and topic are consistent with the verifier's knowledge, exact quotes not confirmed; paraphrase, do not quote]**. THE LINE would stack 9 M people at Lotte-Tower height. The Prieto-Curiel paper adds a related point: "a linear system forms the most vulnerable type of network… fire in any of the stations [could] potentially leav[e] most people static."
- Guardian critic Oliver Wainwright (**27 Jul 2022** — **[VERIFIER: CORRECTED date, previously "2022-08"]**): a "habitable supercomputer" with an "ominous dystopian undertone"; "If ever there was an urban vision that embraced our end-of-days climate apocalypse, then this is it" — [Archinect summary of Guardian review](https://archinect.com/news/article/150323438/oliver-wainwright-explores-the-architectural-history-behind-saudi-arabia-s-planned-the-line-megacity) **[SOURCED; quotes consistent with verifier knowledge and `critique.md` F18, medium]**

## Open questions / caveats
1. Every cited *page* remains unread; only the OWID dataset, the Nature paper text (via cache) and Wikipedia/Census fragments were read directly. Re-open the Korean pages (매일신문, IFEZ, 비즈워치, 디지털타임스, 아시아경제) before any verbatim on-screen quote.
2. Seoul density for 2023/2024 not retrieved; 2022 (15,550/km²) used — 2024 registered population implies ~15,400/km², same 17× headline. Using Seoul's *built-up-area* density (~25,900/km²) would cut the multiple to ~10×.
3. NEOM never published an energy-demand estimate; 120 TWh is per-capita scaling. The two Saudi generation series (OWID 454.6 vs CEIC 402.6 TWh) differ by 13 % — state the source with the percentage. A "Heidelberg University: solar the size of Slovakia" claim (Lampoon) could not be verified.
4. No pricing or rent data ever existed for THE LINE, so "who could afford it" rests on Gulf expat-share analogies.
5. The "2.1 billion birds" figure is the Hahn et al. (2009) estimate for the whole Europe–Africa migration system (cited by the horizon scan), not the Red Sea flyway; BirdLife's ">1.5 M soaring birds" is the flyway number, species count 37 or 39.
6. The official 135 × 800 m modules only sum to 108 km; unclear how the remaining ~62 km was meant to be used.
7. The canyon-light model assumes a straight, empty, exactly E–W or 20°-rotated canyon; a real EIA-grade daylight study for THE LINE was never published.
8. The exact 2025–26 items (Semafor 22 May and 7 Jun 2026, AGBI website comparison) are corroborated only by feed headlines and the sibling sweep; re-read before use.
